## Findings

### [significant] The Lance flotation sentence contradicts itself (§2.7)

With the waterline at 9° N (pole down, 244 m draft), the whole southern band (30°–50° S) is 110–160 m under water: gear bays, ramps and southern batteries. The sentence says the band stays dry, then puts 'the gear' under.

*Suggested resolution:* Read: '…which leaves every hangar mouth dry and puts the southern band, the gear, the entry face and the shield face under.'

### [significant] Flotation figures leave out the drive fairing (§2.7, A.8)

4.87 and 5.62 B m³ are reproduced only by excluding the fairing's 198 M m³ (and its 0.315 M m² of waterplane). With the fairing the displacements are 5.06 and 5.82 B m³. Immersed densities become 0.950 and 0.825 t/m³. Drafts (fresh / sea / brine): flooded 602 / 589 / 501 m, hardened 519 / 506 / 417 m. §2.7 also uses 0.96 t/m³ for the hull; since the fairing was added the figure is 0.93.

*Suggested resolution:* Rebase §2.7 and A.8 on the 4.83 B m³ hull.

### [significant] Praetorian's own deck grid cannot carry the galleries (B.6, B.3)

B.6 anchors each core's internal grid on its own equator. That works for a Lance: the galleries sit 54 m and 120 m above its equator (z −290), 9 and 20 levels. Praetorian's equator is at z −282, so the galleries sit 46 m and 112 m above it. Neither is a whole number of 6 m levels (remainder 4 m), so a gallery can't run into Praetorian without a step. The z +28 command deck is on the hull grid (n = 44), not on an equator-anchored one.

*Suggested resolution:* Anchor Praetorian's grid on the hull grid (a level at z −284, 2 m under its equator). The centre can't move, because the south cap has to stay flush at z −630.

### [significant] The flag command deck is not below the flag plot (B.9, §15)

B.9 puts Praetorian's command deck at z +28, 'one level below the plot'. But the plot spans n = 42–46 (z +16 to +40), and z +28 is n = 44, in the middle of that span. One level below the plot would be z +10 (n = 41). z +28 is also 63.0° N on the core, inside the northern cap that B.6 gives to the drive cluster and its plant (above 60° N).

*Suggested resolution:* Move the command deck to z +10 (n = 41, 57.5° N), just under the drive cap, or move the plot up.

### [significant] Praetorian's keys and hangar mouths share the same eight longitudes (§3.1, B.6)

B.6 clocks all eight keys (50 m wide, 20 m proud) to the web longitudes. It also puts Praetorian's sixteen hangar mouths (60 × 20 m, at 7.6° N and 18.8° N) 'on the spur longitudes', which are the same longitudes. Each key channel would run through two mouths, and each spur would enter the bowl through the key. On a Lance the two are 22.5° apart, so the conflict is Praetorian's alone.

*Suggested resolution:* Clock Praetorian's keys to 22.5° + 45n, or split each key above 22° N. The model uses the split key.

### [significant] Praetorian's thruster wells can't be evenly spaced (§3.3, B.6)

There are 48 wells a belt at ±8°. Evenly spaced on 7.5°, the wells nearest each spur longitude sit 3.75° off it, inside the 60 m mouth at 7.6° N, which spans ±4.98° of longitude. So even spacing would place wells inside mouths, contrary to 'never share a longitude with one'.

*Suggested resolution:* Group the wells six to each 35° gap between mouths (≈5.8° spacing). The model does this.

### [significant] Three fittings claim the dorsal r 1,119 circle (§6, §7, B.8)

The dorsal two-thirds medium band puts 234 mounts on a 30 m pitch at r 1,119, which uses 7020 m of the 7031 m circumference. Section 6 puts the 8 dorsal shield rings (60 m apertures) at two-thirds radius, and §7 puts 16 dorsal sensors (20 × 8 m) there too. That circle has 11 m to spare. The one-third circle has the same problem: 117 mounts use 3510 of 3515 m, and 16 more sensors are specified on it. The HTML datum model draws all of these on the same circles.

*Suggested resolution:* Offset the emitters and sensors radially clear of the mount swept envelopes. The model puts the emitters at r 1,175 (inside their generator hall at r 1,019–1,219), the two-thirds sensors at r 1,060 and the one-third sensors at r 505.

### [significant] The ordnance cells break through the dish at their inboard foot (B.8, B.10)

The ordnance blocks run from r 1,050 at levels n = −40 to −10, so their floor is at z −476. At r 1,050 the ventral moulded surface is at z −470 (the crown edge), and it only drops below −476 at r ≈ 1,072 (below −480, which clears the 4 m grade, at r ≈ 1,078). The inboard 22 m of the blocks' bottom level sits outside the hull, opening the magazines onto the cavity. That is the most heavily protected volume on the ship and the bearing it is most exposed on. Neither the Annex B.13 check nor the HTML model finds it, because both sample at up to 40 m.

*Suggested resolution:* Start the ordnance blocks at r 1,080, or raise their floor to n = −38 (z −464).

### [significant] The growing decks' z range holds 14 tiers, not 16 (B.3, B.11)

n = −25 to −18 and z −386 to −344 span 42 m (seven pitches). Sixteen 3 m tiers, or 'eight primary bays', need 48 m. C.12's ordnance-block volume (71 M m³) reproduces only if a range 'n = a to b' means deck planes a to b (−476 to −296), which confirms the 42 m reading. At 14 tiers the growing area is 515,200 × 14 = 7.21 M m², short of the 8.25 M m² §12 requires.
